# Q1 draft notes

- Gamification can turn rewarded behaviours into shared workplace expectations. Case F links games with learning, individualism, fun and business performance. [Case F, pp. 1-2; L6, p. 4]
- Enterprising culture may strengthen. Games use rewards and competition, while game-based problem solving can develop creativity and persistence. Achievement and creative thinking may become more valued. [Case F, p. 2; L6, p. 4]
- Social culture may strengthen. IBM uses games to connect remote and travelling colleagues. Interaction may support belonging and collaboration; increased trust is not demonstrated. [Case F, p. 2; L6, p. 4]
- Learning may become a stronger cultural value. Marriott uses games for employee training; Siemens helps employees understand their roles. Making development engaging may encourage employees to treat learning as part of work. [Case F, p. 1]
- Rewards communicate priorities. SAP awards points for environmental tasks such as carpooling. Recognition may make social responsibility a more valued part of everyday work. [Case F, p. 2; L6, p. 9]
- A control-oriented culture may also develop. Disney's laundry workers felt compelled to compete and called productivity tracking an "electronic whip". Pressure may normalise competition and weaken trust. [Case F, p. 3]
- Overall, the culture strengthened depends on the behaviours encouraged. The case stresses aligning employee goals with business goals; gamification cannot compensate for poor management. [Case F, p. 2]
