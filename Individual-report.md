# Individual reflection on the Case F group project

In Group 20, we studied how gamification is used in the workplace. My main contribution was Q1, which asked how gamification could shape organisational culture. Dividing the work and reviewing the drafts helped us finish on time. Volunteering for the final audit made me think more about how the sections fit together and what a reader needs from the whole report. ([Case F - Gamification.pdf, p. 3](<Case F - Gamification.pdf#page=3>))

## Planning

We spent about two weeks drafting and another week refining the report. We gave feedback at the end of both phases. The planning lecture describes a plan as the steps needed to reach an objective. For us, the first review gave us a goal to work towards before the submission deadline. We knew who was responsible for each question, and we still had about a week to make changes after sharing our drafts. Next time, I would also make time at the start to agree on what each answer should cover. ([ENG3003_L5_Ch8_Fundamentals of Planning.pdf, p. 5](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L5_Ch8_Fundamentals of Planning.pdf#page=5>))

## Organising

We divided the questions together. I was responsible for Q1 and helped with the first drafts of Q3 and Q4. The shared document let us see how other sections were developing, and we discussed changes in an online meeting. This helped us coordinate the work we had divided up. In the finished report, Q1 uses IBM and Disney to explain how gamification might strengthen or weaken a culture that values collaboration and trust. Q3 uses both examples to compare game features that help colleagues connect or put pressure on workers. Agreeing on those different purposes earlier could help us decide where to explain the examples in detail and where a short reference back would be enough. ([ENG3003_L5_Ch 11 Fundamentals of Organizing.pdf, p. 5](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L5_Ch 11 Fundamentals of Organizing.pdf#page=5>), [Group 20 report, Q1](group-report.md#question-1) and [Q3](group-report.md#question-3))

## Controlling

We checked our progress after drafting and reviewed the report again after refinement. The first review was a form of concurrent control because we could still act on the feedback while the work was in progress. When I checked the final document, I still found citations to fix and formatting to tidy up. I took corrective action by adding or correcting citations and making the formatting more consistent. We submitted on time. Having a progress check during drafting and a quality check before submission gave us time to deal with issues as they came up. ([ENG3003_L6_Ch 9 Fundamentals of Control.pdf, p. 6](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L6_Ch 9 Fundamentals of Control.pdf#page=6>) and [p. 18](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L6_Ch 9 Fundamentals of Control.pdf#page=18>))

## Leading

One person took the lead during drafting and refinement and helped with discussion and feedback. That gave us a way to talk through changes for the next version. I see my decision to volunteer for the final audit as an example of distributed leadership. The teamwork lecture explains that members can lead by noticing work that needs doing and taking it on. One person gave us direction through both phases, and I took responsibility for a task that affected the whole group. ([ENG3003_L9_Ch 17 Teams and Teamwork.pdf, p. 25](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L9_Ch 17 Teams and Teamwork.pdf#page=25>))

## What I learned from the final audit

When I wrote Q1, I had to decide which case examples would help me explain how gamification might affect workplace culture. During the final audit, I read the report from the point of view of someone seeing all the answers for the first time. A citation mattered because it let that reader find the evidence behind a claim. Consistent formatting helped show which points belonged under each heading. By working on those details across the report, I saw that my role also included helping the reader make sense of work written by several people. ([Group 20 report, Q1](group-report.md#question-1))

The control lecture describes setting standards for the quality of finished work. For our report, I would ask whether a reader could find the source for a case claim wherever it appears. A final reviewer can add a missing citation when the source is clear and make the layout consistent. If I am unsure whether a source supports a claim, I would ask the person who wrote it to explain how they used the evidence. That conversation needs time. On another project, I would raise those questions at the first review so that the writers can deal with them during refinement. I would keep a final check of how the sections fit together. ([ENG3003_L6_Ch 9 Fundamentals of Control.pdf, p. 14](<lecture-slides/ENG3003_L6_Ch 9 Fundamentals of Control.pdf#page=14>))

In my next engineering group project, I would keep the shared document and the two reviews. Before we start drafting, I would ask each person to write one sentence in the document explaining what their section needs to establish and how it connects to any related section.
